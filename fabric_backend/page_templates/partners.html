What you bring

  • one bounded area and one question in your own words, such as the orders and invoices behind a monthly figure
  • access to the data: a read-only sign-in, an export of the tables (Excel or CSV), or to begin with only the column names
  • one person who knows the concepts: what an order, a customer or an amount means in your business

No customer data is needed for a first conversation; the column names are enough to start.

What you get

  • a picture of how the tables connect, with the rule behind each link
  • documented checks against the data (keys, references, amounts at the wrong level) and a list of open questions
  • when the material is sufficient, a read-only overview: a data dictionary, views or a semantic model draft built from the reviewed plan

When it is done

The question can be answered against an agreed reference, such as a figure finance already reports, and someone on your side who knows the concepts signs it off.

Until then, the open questions say what is missing and who can supply it.

First assessment

From what you bring, often in one meeting: we read the material with {{PRODUCT}}, run the checks it allows, and end with one of five answers: enough to start, we need your knowledge about something, material is missing, access is missing, or it is not supported yet. No percentage of readiness, and nothing is built.

Delivery

Once the assessment says there is enough to start: connect to the data, review the relationships together, settle the open questions with your person who knows the concepts, and build the chosen read-only result from the plan you agreed on. It is done when the question checks out against the reference and is signed off.

How a pilot could run
