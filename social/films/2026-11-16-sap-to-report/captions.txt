=== (c) LinkedIn ===

SAP data in Microsoft Fabric often arrives as hundreds of tables with short, cryptic names. VBAK, VBAP, KNA1, LIKP, VBRK: how do they connect, and which of them belong together?

This 55-second film shows what Krusgraph does with them, on a synthetic, SAP-shaped example:

1. It finds the links between the tables (order line to order, order to customer, deliveries, invoices) and checks them against the rows: 32 of 34 links hold, and a link that does not is flagged.
2. It suggests data products from those links: Order to cash and Customer master, each with its tables in plain words and an owner still to be assigned.
3. It builds a semantic model from Order to cash, with facts, dimensions and measures that count each order's total once.
4. A report on that model shows order value, top customers and billing.

Every name and number in the film comes from Krusgraph's engine run on the synthetic sample; nothing comes from an SAP system. Voice: AI-generated. Krusgraph is independent and not affiliated with SAP or Microsoft.

You can try the check on your own tables in your browser: krusgraph.com/check
