Every link on the map names its rule. Then the rows test it.

A data model is only as good as its links. So we show two things for each one:

The rule. In our example shop, orders.customer_id points at customers by rule R1: a column named after a table that has a single key.

The count. The engine counts the link in the rows: "holds for all 60 rows with a value". Whole sample: 6 of 6 keys unique, 6 of 6 relationships hold without orphans.

And when the rows disagree, it says so. In our SAP-shaped example, the link sap_vbap → sap_vbak comes from the SAP pack, and the rows answer: "1 of 6 rows (16.7%) point at nothing". One order line points at an order that is not there.

A check that only says "all good" is hard to trust. This one says what held, what did not, and what was not counted.
