Meet Thinker. Thinker counts against the rows.

"I tell you what held, what did not, and what I did not count."

Onto proposes the links. Thinker checks them: is every key unique, and does every row point at something that exists? Then it says which links need a look, and which amounts a join would count twice.

In our SAP-shaped example: 17 of 17 keys unique, 32 of 34 links hold. Of the two that do not, one has a row that points at nothing, and one has nothing to check yet.

It counts over whole tables, up to 60 keys and links per call (200 at most), and lists what it did not count. No score out of 100: counts you can read.
